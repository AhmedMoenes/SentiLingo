1. Project Planning & Management

What SentiLingo is, what it will deliver and when, how the team works, which risks matter most and how success is measured.

1.1 Project Overview

Companies receive customer feedback through app and e-commerce reviews, social media, surveys and support tickets. In Egypt this feedback is commonly written in Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), Egyptian Arabic and English, and may mix Arabic and English in one message. Tagging it by hand is slow and inconsistent, and many ready-made tools do not report how well they handle Egyptian Arabic (see Part 2).

Problem statement (from the project brief). A company receives customer feedback in natural-language text and needs to automatically identify whether feedback expresses positive, negative, or other predefined sentiment categories.

SentiLingo labels each piece of feedback Negative, Neutral or Positive with an estimated confidence. It compares a dummy classifier and focused classical baselines using word, character and combined TF-IDF with Multinomial Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression and Linear SVM. XLM-R Base is the required advanced model; MARBERT is an Arabic-only stretch experiment and BiLSTM is included only if the instructor requires it. Models are selected using the mean of the English, MSA and Egyptian Arabic macro-F1 scores on validation data. The locked configuration is then evaluated once on the frozen test set.

1.3 Scope

In scope

  • English, Modern Standard Arabic and Egyptian Arabic
  • Three classes: Negative, Neutral, Positive
  • Public, labelled review and tweet data
  • Word, character and combined TF-IDF with dummy, NB, LR and Linear SVM baselines
  • XLM-R Base, reproducible experiment records, evaluation and error analysis
  • Local Streamlit application with single-text and CSV prediction

Out of scope

  • Aspect-based sentiment and emotion detection
  • Speech, image or video feedback
  • Live social-media crawling and CRM integration
  • Collecting private customer data
  • Production SLAs and automated retraining
  • Franco-Arabic and Arabic dialects other than Egyptian Arabic
  • User accounts, authentication and a production database
  • FastAPI, Docker and Azure unless the core release is complete

Assumptions and constraints

  • The five official team members have limited availability, so the plan protects a small required release and uses one owner and one reviewer for each deliverable.
  • Zero budget: local resources, free Colab or Kaggle GPUs, and open-source tools; Azure remains optional.
  • Only public data is used, and no personal data is committed to the repository.
  • The local RTX 4050 with 6 GB VRAM is used for smoke tests and inference; hosted GPUs are used for the main Transformer runs.
  • The active five-week delivery period starts on 2 November and the final presentation is on 4 December 2026.

1.5 Methodology

The project follows CRISP-DM (Wirth & Hipp, 2000): business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation and deployment. The work is compressed into five weekly delivery cycles. Each cycle begins with a short planning meeting, assigns one owner and one reviewer to every deliverable, and ends with evidence-based review. GitHub issues track work, reviewed pull requests protect the main branch, and every reported result records its data version, configuration, seed, hardware, duration, metrics and artefact location.

1.8 Risk Management

Risks are reviewed at every sprint review. The level combines likelihood and impact before mitigation.

IDRiskLevelMitigation
R1Too little labelled Egyptian-dialect customer feedbackHighCombine several public Arabic sources; label a small gold set by hand; report the dialect slice separately
R2A dataset is withdrawn or has a restrictive licenceHighCheck licences in week 1; keep backup sources; commit download scripts, not raw data
R3Limited GPU memory, hosted-GPU quota or interrupted sessionsHighUse local CPU for classical work, local GPU for smoke tests and inference, hosted GPU for main runs, small batches and saved checkpoints
R4Data leakage: duplicates across splits, or preprocessing fitted on all dataHighDe-duplicate before splitting; scikit-learn Pipelines fitted on training data only; frozen test set
R5Inconsistent labels across sources, and too few Neutral examplesMediumWritten label-mapping policy; manual audit; stratified splits; class weights; macro-F1 as the main metric
R6Egyptian spelling variation, mixed text, sarcasm and negation cause errorsMediumArabic normalisation, character n-grams, language-specific evaluation, difficult-example audit and explicit limitations
R7Uneven availability of team members and delayed reviewsHighOne owner and one reviewer per deliverable, small tasks, early escalation and required-work stop rules
R8Optional API, Docker, Azure or extra models consume the final weekMediumKeep Streamlit local-first and start optional work only after evaluation, documentation and rehearsal are safe
