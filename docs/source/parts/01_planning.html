1. Project Planning & Management

What SentiLingo is, what it will deliver and when, how the team works, which risks matter most and how success is measured.

1.1 Project Overview

Companies receive customer feedback through app and e-commerce reviews, social media, surveys and support tickets. In Egypt this feedback is written in Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), Egyptian Arabic, English and Franco-Arabic (Arabic in Latin letters, e.g. el delivery kan 3ady), often mixed in one message. Tagging it by hand is slow and inconsistent, and many ready-made tools are tuned for English or MSA (see Part 2).

Problem statement (from the project brief). A company receives customer feedback in natural-language text and needs to automatically identify whether feedback expresses positive, negative, or other predefined sentiment categories.

SentiLingo labels each piece of feedback Negative, Neutral or Positive with a confidence score. It compares three model families on the same data splits: a classical baseline (TF-IDF with word and character n-grams, Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression), a BiLSTM, and a fine-tuned pretrained Transformer. All experiments are tracked in MLflow. The best model is chosen by macro-F1 on a frozen test set, its errors are analysed, and it can optionally be served from Azure Machine Learning.

1.3 Scope

In scope

  • Arabic (MSA, Egyptian) and English; Franco-Arabic evaluated where data allows
  • Three classes: Negative, Neutral, Positive
  • Public, labelled review and tweet data
  • Preprocessing, TF-IDF and n-grams, NB, LR, BiLSTM, Transformer
  • Evaluation, MLflow tracking, error analysis, sample predictions
  • Optional: Azure ML endpoint and Streamlit demo

Out of scope

  • Aspect-based sentiment and emotion detection
  • Speech, image or video feedback
  • Live social-media crawling and CRM integration
  • Collecting private customer data
  • Production SLAs and automated retraining

Assumptions and constraints

  • Zero budget: free Colab and Kaggle GPUs, open-source tools, and student or DEPI Azure credit for the optional deployment.
  • Only public data is used, and no personal data is committed to the repository.
  • Base-size Transformers with inputs of up to 128 tokens fit the free GPUs.
  • The dates are fixed: kick-off on 19 October and final presentation on 4 December 2026.

1.5 Methodology

The project follows CRISP-DM (Wirth & Hipp, 2000): business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation and deployment. These phases run as seven one-week sprints. Each sprint starts with a planning meeting and ends with a short review. Tasks live on a GitHub Projects board, code is merged through reviewed pull requests, and every reported result links to its MLflow run.

1.8 Risk Management

Risks are reviewed at every sprint review. The level combines likelihood and impact before mitigation.

IDRiskLevelMitigation
R1Too little labelled Egyptian-dialect customer feedbackHighCombine several public Arabic sources; label a small gold set by hand; report the dialect slice separately
R2A dataset is withdrawn or has a restrictive licenceHighCheck licences in week 1; keep backup sources; commit download scripts, not raw data
R3Limited free GPU timeHighBase or distilled models, inputs of up to 128 tokens, mixed precision, saved checkpoints
R4Data leakage: duplicates across splits, or preprocessing fitted on all dataHighDe-duplicate before splitting; scikit-learn Pipelines fitted on training data only; frozen test set
R5Inconsistent labels across sources, and too few Neutral examplesMediumWritten label-mapping policy; manual audit; stratified splits; class weights; macro-F1 as the main metric
R6Dialect, Franco-Arabic, sarcasm and negation cause errorsMediumArabic normalisation, character n-grams, dialect-aware models, error-analysis categories
R7Uneven availability of team membersMediumWeekly planning, early warning of delays, shared tasks, buffer in the final week
R8Azure cost or quota problems for the optional deploymentLowSmallest suitable instance, deleted after the demo; local Streamlit demo as fallback
